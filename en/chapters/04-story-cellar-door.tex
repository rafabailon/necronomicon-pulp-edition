\ChapterPage{Page 4 \textbullet{} The Story}{The Door Below the Stairs}
The customs house smelled of wet paper and extinguished candles. A brass
key, number 9, opened the cellar door without turning. The lock simply
clicked when Mara held the key near it. Dr. Voss made a note and did not
write down what the note meant.

At the foot of the stairs, seawater covered the floor by a finger's breadth.
It moved toward a stone arch in the eastern wall. Beyond the arch was no
room shown on the building plan, only a narrow passage lined with green
glass. Each pane reflected the pair from a different year: Mara as a child,
Voss with a beard gone white, both of them wearing coats they did not own.

The chart in the bottle warmed in Mara's hands. A new line appeared, ink
spreading through the paper without a pen. It traced the passage and stopped
at a circle beneath the harbor. From above came three measured strokes of
the bell.

They did not count the fourth. The stone arch widened, and the tide began
to climb the stairs. Mara folded the chart along its old creases. The drawn
coastline vanished. The real one did not.
