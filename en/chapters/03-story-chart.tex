\ChapterPage{Pages 3--4 \textbullet{} The Story}{The Chart in the Bottle}
\lettrine[lines=3,loversize=0.12]{W}{hen} Mara Bell found the bottle, the
tide was running inland. She stood on the black shingle below Greyhaven,
where the stones clicked together like teeth in a cold mouth. The bottle
rested above the foam, dry as a bone, and its cork bore a neat brass seal.

Inside lay a chart of the coast, drawn with a sailor's confidence and a
surveyor's care. Greyhaven was marked, as were the reef, the lighthouse, and
the old customs house. Beyond them, in water no chart admitted, someone had
inked a second coastline. It curved around the harbor like an enormous
closed hand.

Mara had inherited her father's map case and his habit of checking every
measurement twice. She measured the distance from the customs house to the
newly drawn shore. The scale gave nine miles. The actual distance was the
width of the harbor.

Dr. Voss arrived carrying a lantern and a satchel of notebooks. He did not
ask where she had found the bottle. He asked whether it had been warm.
Mara answered that the glass was cold. The doctor looked disappointed, then
afraid, as though the bottle had failed a test he had hoped it would pass.
