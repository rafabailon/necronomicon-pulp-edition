\section*{From the Night Editor}
The Chronicle wishes to amend yesterday's report. The harbor bell was not
heard at half past three. It was heard at three minutes before four, though
all three witnesses insist that the clock in the square stopped at 3:33.

The distinction matters to no practical purpose. Nevertheless, the editor
has spent the morning comparing watches, and none agrees with the town clock.
Its hands move correctly when observed from the street. Viewed through the
glass, they appear to point toward the floor.

\textit{Lorem tenebris}: darkness is not the absence of a lamp but the
interval in which a lamp remembers its first flame. This sentence was found
in the margin of a tide book dated 1792. No one at the archive recognizes
the handwriting.

\section*{Lost and Found}
One brass key, stamped with the number 9, was found on the eastern quay. It
fits no door in the customs house, though the lock on the cellar entrance
has recently been polished. Anyone claiming it must describe the pattern
on its teeth without looking directly at them.

\section*{A Statement from the Lighthouse}
Keeper Wren reports that the lamp made one revolution too many last night.
The mechanism has been examined and is in good repair. Wren has requested
that the shutters remain closed during daylight, a request the board has
declined.

At dawn, a second shadow was visible on the water beneath the lighthouse.
It did not correspond to any object above the waves. The shadow turned
counterclockwise while the sun rose in the east.

\section*{Advertisement}
\textsc{Morrow \& Sons} offers dependable clocks, barometers, and marine
chronometers. Every instrument is tested against three independent standards
and one honest moon. No purchase includes a guarantee against irregular tides.

\textbf{Editor's note:} the printer asks that the final column be left
blank. The request is denied. A newspaper with an empty column invites
letters to the editor, and we have already received one from the sea.
